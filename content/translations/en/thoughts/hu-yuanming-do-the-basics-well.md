Hu Yuanming: Find a problem that is important and interesting enough, then do the most fundamental things really well.

1. **Thoroughness comes from loving what you do, rather than forcing yourself to keep going.** (Reflection 44) Among the “geniuses” he observed at Tsinghua and MIT, the most important trait was a love for their work. For people who are “hardworking + smart,” effort is draining: “Oh, let's leave it at that. I just can't keep going.” People who are “hardworking + smart + passionate” think, “I can make this a little better, because I'm not done playing yet.” That love also gives them the patience to attend to details others find tedious: rigorous experiments, and even getting every punctuation mark in a paper right.

2. **Set priorities first, then pursue excellence.** (Reflection 45) The best people are exacting, but they know their priorities and do the important things first. Only after the fundamental problems have been solved do they turn to polishing the details.

3. **The “basics” are themselves difficult.** (Reflection 46) Know what you actually want. Do what you say you will do. Go to bed on time. Think independently. Make sure readers can understand what you write. Avoid empty platitudes. Use your own product every day. Put facts ahead of saving face. He believes doing these things well is enough to put you in the top 1%. The difficulty is actually doing them.

4. **Focus. Do fewer things.** (Reflection 47) “Since doing the basics well is already hard, let's avoid trying to do too many things at once.” Choosing what not to do can mean gaining something, rather than losing it.

5. **Clear expression is a test of clear thinking.** (Reflection 17) “When you cannot express something clearly, the problem often lies in unclear thinking rather than a lack of communication skills.”

6. **Without solid fundamentals, AI only amplifies confusion.** (Reflections 16 and 18) AI completely decouples “form” from “content,” making it easy to produce something that looks convincing but falls apart under scrutiny. His company still places considerable weight on candidates' ability to write code by hand without AI during interviews.

7. **Keep expectations low, and commit wholeheartedly.** (Reflection 40) “Even if failure is the most likely outcome, I still put my whole heart into it. Failure is okay; success would be wonderful.”
