---
title: "A new Agent Harness idea: an agent should hand you toilet paper when you need it"
description: "A human-centered Agent Harness should prepare the environment and remove friction, helping people carry out the meaningful work themselves."
---

I am trying to define a human-centered Agent Harness. Current harnesses such as Codex and Claude Code emphasize an agent’s ability to run for long periods without intervention. Their design philosophy is to take over human work and deliver results from end to end without human involvement. The recent enthusiasm for loop engineering and self-evolving agents points toward the same goal: less and less human intervention, until the agent runs automatically. This philosophy of removing people from the workflow is useful for things people do not want to do. For things they truly care about, however, it takes away their control over and participation in what gives them meaning. I think we need a new agent paradigm that collaborates better with people. It would be deeply human-centered: rather than replacing the work a person needs to do, it would offer necessary assistance, including help the person has not yet realized they need, so they can grow through that help.

Here is a somewhat crude metaphor: **handing you toilet paper while you take a shit.** With the current Agent Harness, a person wants to take a shit, so the agent does it for them and asks them to approve that the shit is theirs. With a human-centered harness, the person wants to take a shit, so the agent gets the toilet and paper ready, hands them their phone, and lets them get on with it comfortably. No approval or consent step is needed.

Much of what is called human-in-the-loop is really this: the agent does the person’s work, then demotes the person to an approval button. That still takes away the person’s central action. They appear to be in the loop, but they are no longer in the work. This wastes tokens. For a task someone cares about, any AI-generated content that fails to move them or win their own agreement wastes tokens. I can ask AI to generate the core features of this kind of harness, but if the output does not resonate with me or help my subsequent design thinking, those tokens have been entirely wasted. A human-centered harness is closer to this:

> **The agent does not perform the central action in the person’s place. It arranges an environment that lets the person perform that action more smoothly.**

The real difference is that a current harness performs the central task for a person and then asks them to approve or reject it. A human-centered harness prepares the environment, materials, tools, pace, and conditions for resuming work, while the person carries out the meaningful central action.

This also means that “human-centered” does not mean asking people more questions or requesting more approvals. That merely makes the dispossession more polite. Truly human-centered work means **people do not need to approve what they are already doing, because they are the ones doing it.**

Take the Founder Agent I am currently working on. The wrong form is:

> A founder wants to judge whether an idea is worth pursuing. The agent completes the analysis, delivers a conclusion, and asks the founder to approve it.

The right form is:

> A founder wants to assess an idea. The agent organizes interview materials and brings earlier lines of thought into view, so the founder can smoothly mark contradictions, organize product hypotheses, analyze next actions, and enter into the judgment themselves.

Features such as making evidence clearer or exposing assumptions do not matter in themselves. What matters is whether they serve this principle:

> **The central action is irreplaceable: do not replace it; remove the friction around it.**

This has substantial implications for harness design:

- Sometimes the best AI output is no output, just having the materials ready.
- Sometimes the best automation helps a person enter a productive state rather than completing the task.
- The worst waste of tokens is AI rushing to generate a pile of content that is “correct but leaves you unmoved.”
- Evaluation should consider whether the person completed their own task more smoothly, alongside whether the agent completed a task.
- Participation is evidence of agency; approval alone is not.

So I would redefine this harness as follows:

> A Human-centered Agent Harness provides environmental scaffolding. Its goal is to preserve the person’s central action, reduce surrounding friction, and strengthen their judgment and participation, rather than completing the task end to end in their place.

This is a little more accurate than “judgment-preserving.” It protects the person’s central action, of which judgment is only one kind. Writing, research, entrepreneurship, learning, creation, conversation, and choosing a direction in life can all be central actions of this kind.
