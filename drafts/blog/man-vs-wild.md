## 一、Hidi & Renninger (2006)：兴趣怎么长出来

[《The Four-Phase Model of Interest Development》](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4)（*Educational Psychologist*）把兴趣拆成四个**阶段**（phases，不是不可逆的台阶；没有支持就会掉回去或休眠）：

**第 1 阶段：Triggered situational interest（被触发的情境兴趣）**  
你被某个内容短暂抓住注意力。可能开心，也可能只是新奇甚至带点不适。你未必清楚地意识到“我对这个有兴趣”。通常需要别人或环境推一把（一组对话、一个 demo、一个写好的小任务）。  
→ 这就是你的 `ok interesting`。

**第 2 阶段：Maintained situational interest（被维持的情境兴趣）**  
你愿意**再碰**同一种内容。有人帮你把这件事和你已有的技能、经历连上。情绪偏正面，知识开始攒，也开始觉得“这东西好像有点价值”。还主要靠外部撑着。

**第 3 阶段：Emerging individual interest（正在成形的个人兴趣）**  
你开始**自己**回头找这件事。冒出自己的好奇心问题，能自己管一点注意力。你有了存下来的知识和价值感，但可能对“学科正统”和别人的反馈还不太在乎，更在意自己的问题。

**第 4 阶段：Well-developed individual interest（充分发展的个人兴趣）**  
你持续自己回来，会改问题、找答案。面对挫折和难度还能撑住。你会主动要反馈，也认得这个领域里别人的贡献。  
→ 这才接近你要的 `I must do that`。

Renninger 后来的讲义（[STELAR 讲义](https://stelar.edc.org/sites/default/files/2022-11/Day%202_Ann%20Renninger%20.pdf)）还补了一点：兴趣有两层意思，一是**当下投入时的心理状态**，二是**长期愿意再回来**的倾向。热爱属于后者，而且要靠互动和环境设计慢慢堆；不是某个早晨突然揭晓的身份。

## 二、O’Keefe, Dweck & Walton (2018)：你怎么想“兴趣从哪来”，会改写发展过程

[《Implicit Theories of Interest: Finding Your Passion or Developing It?》](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618780643)（*Psychological Science*；开放获取：[PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6180666/)）问的是另一件事：人们私下相信兴趣是**固定的**（fixed：核心兴趣早就在那儿，等你发现）还是**可发展的**（growth：兴趣是投入之后长出来的）。这篇是五组实验，不是口号。

**Studies 1–3（开放性）**  
大学生先标明自己偏 techy 还是 fuzzy，再各读一篇对口、一篇不对口的文章。越信“兴趣固定”，对**圈外**那篇兴趣越低；圈内那篇两种信念差不多。Study 3 用假新闻式文章**诱导**信念，因果方向也成立：被诱导成固定观的人，对圈外话题兴趣更低。  
含义：固定观会让你更早收窄探索面。

**Study 4（对动机的预期）**  
越信固定观，越容易说：一旦找到激情，就会有**无限动机**，也不太承认追求激情时会难。成长观的人更常预期：就算热爱，有时也会难。

**Study 5（难度来了会怎样，最关键）**  
先诱导信念，再看一段有趣的黑洞科普视频（多数人说 fascinating），然后读一篇很难的 *Science* 原文。结果：两边在视频后兴趣都很高；读完难文后，**固定观组掉得更狠**。尤其觉得文章难的人：固定观组兴趣预测均值约 2.75（6 分制，低于中点），成长观组约 3.59。  
作者的解释：固定观默认“真爱该自带源源动力”；一碰到摩擦，就解读成“原来这不是我的兴趣”。

## How to Work Hard

1. 出色的工作需要天赋、练习和努力。 脚注里他改口说，更早的配方是天赋、决心和兴趣；决心加上兴趣，才会变成练习和努力。兴趣是因，努力是果。
2. 学校里的「工作」常常是假的。 科目被改造成适合教小孩的样子，和从业者真正做的事差很远。G. H. Hardy 小时候对数学没有热爱，只想靠考试赢过别人；读到 Jordan 的《分析教程》才第一次知道数学是什么。Graham 的结论是：多数人要先认识工作的形状，才会爱它。
3. 适合做什么，兴趣比天赋更要紧。 深的兴趣能让人比任何纪律都更用力。兴趣比天赋更难发现：天赋种类少，小时候就被评过；兴趣很细，可能到二十多岁才成熟，题目本身也可能那时才存在。
4. 兴趣很容易被冒充。 要分清：你是真的对这件事有兴趣，还是因为能赚钱、能被人佩服、能让父母满意。赚钱可以是清醒的动机，危险的是它在不知不觉中改写你对「什么有趣」的的判断。
5. 不是每个人都有召唤。 电影和精简传记让人以为人人都有一个 calling，找出来就行。Mozart 是这样，Newton 不是：他在数学、炼金术、神学之间转来转去，事后被说成「天命」只是后见之明。对后一种人，选方向本身就是努力工作的一部分。判断要不要换方向，最好的测试是你是否觉得它有趣——前提是你对自己诚实。



### How to Do What You Love

The definition of work was now to make some original contribution to the world, and in the process not to starve. But after the habit of so many years my idea of work still included a large component of pain. Work still seemed to require discipline, because only hard problems yielded grand results, and hard problems couldn't literally be fun. Surely one had to force oneself to work on them.

上限：不是「这一秒最想做什么」。谁都会有片刻宁愿去罗马闲逛，而不是做难题。这句话说的是一周、一个月这种长度上，什么让你最快乐。纯消费的快乐很快会腻。

下限：你对工作的喜欢，要超过任何没有产出的快乐，以至于「业余时间」这个概念本身显得不对。工作累了可以去做点没脑子的事，但那段时间不是奖品，工作时间也不是为了换奖品而忍受的痛苦。低于这条线，你就得逼自己，做出来的东西会明显差一截。

还要能佩服自己做的事。做完能说「这挺酷」。所以必须有一个检验：Graham 借来的标准是，做出会让你佩服的朋友说「哇」的东西。光读书通常不够，因为读得好不好没有检验；你得用读到的东西做出点什么。

检验是：就算没人付钱，甚至得另打一份工来养活自己，你还会不会做这件事。好的数学家会；光谱另一端的许多学术工作不会，那些论文是在竞争教职，不是因为有人觉得好玩。

找到热爱的工作很难，几十亿人里也许只有几十万做到。承认自己不满意，已经比还在自欺的大多数人超前一步。做出色的工作需要的纪律比人想的少，因为办法就是找到一件喜欢到不必强迫自己的事；但寻找本身通常需要纪律。

两条自检：

- 就算不喜欢手头的事，也把它做好。这样你知道自己不是拿不满当懒惰的借口，同时也养成把事情做好的习惯。
- 一直在产出。 白天的工作你不认真，是因为打算当小说家，那你现在有没有在写？只要在写，那个遥远的伟大小说就不会变成麻醉剂。「一直产出」还会自动把你从「应该做的事」推向「实际上喜欢的事」。

知道自己想做什么，和现在就能做，是两件事。有野心的人得刻意把它们分开，否则会提前降低期望，用「我不会画画」来避免正视失败。

养活自己有两条路：

- 有机路线： 把工作做好，逐渐增加你喜欢的部分、减少不喜欢的部分。常见，但慢，而且不确定。它能把你从建筑带到产品设计，很难带到音乐。
- 两份工作： 用不喜欢的事赚钱，来供养喜欢的事。可以是日常的 day job，也可以是先赚够再退出。它能跳过职业之间的墙，但更危险：生活会变贵，人会被吸在赚钱的工作上；枯燥的工作做得太久会腐蚀判断；高薪工作最危险，因为它占满注意力。

工作完全可以是你喜欢的事。只是这里的“喜欢”不是喜欢一个职业名称，也不是喜欢它带来的收入、身份和声望，而是：

- 你喜欢它实际要做的那些事；
- 你佩服做出来的东西；
- 做完以后，你会觉得“这挺酷”；
- 在一周、一个月这样的时间尺度上，你愿意把时间给它，而不是总想逃到别处。

能忍受痛苦，本身不能证明热爱。一个人可能因为野心、恐惧、声望或已经投入太多，而在一份讨厌的工作里坚持很多年。所以，判断标准不是“我能不能忍”，而是：

- 没有外部奖励时，我还会不会主动回来？
- 做出成果时，我感到的是佩服和满足，还是仅仅松了一口气，庆幸终于结束了？
- 我喜欢的是做这件事的过程，还是喜欢别人眼中的这个身份？

他其实讲了三种不同的“痛”，不能混在一起：

1. 困难带来的痛
  题本身难，想不通、做不出、反复失败。这种痛可以出现在你真正喜欢的工作里。
2. 疲劳带来的痛
  子累了，注意力下降，需要休息。这种痛说明你该停一停，不说明你选错了。
3. 厌恶这件事本身带来的痛
  觉得它无聊、虚假、没有意义，或者你只是为了钱和声望在做。长期来看，你只想逃离它。这种痛才是在提示：方向可能错了。

“就算不喜欢手头的事，也把它做好”，不是劝人长期忍受，而是把它当成一个排除干扰的实验：

如果你做得很敷衍，你就分不清：

- 你是真的不喜欢这件事；
- 还是你只是不喜欢自己做不好；
- 还是你本来就懒，不愿付出努力；
- 还是你没有能力，所以一直在受挫。



### The Bus Ticket Theory of Genius

Everyone knows that to do great work you need both natural ability and determination. But there's a third ingredient that's not as well understood: an obsessive interest in a particular topic.

If I had to put the recipe for genius into one sentence, that might be it: to have a disinterested obsession with something that matters.

An obsessive interest in a topic is both a proxy for ability and a substitute for determination.

An obsessive interest will even bring you luck, to the extent anything can. Chance, as Pasteur said, favors the prepared mind, and if there's one thing an obsessed mind is, it's prepared.

The bus ticket theory is similar to Carlyle's famous definition of genius as an infinite capacity for taking pains. But there are two differences. The bus ticket theory makes it clear that the source of this infinite capacity for taking pains is not infinite diligence, as Carlyle seems to have meant, but the sort of infinite interest that collectors have. It also adds an important qualification: an infinite capacity for taking pains about something that matters.

I've always liked ++[Hamming's](https://paulgraham.com/hamming.html)++ famous double-barrelled question: what are the most important problems in your field, and why aren't you working on one of them? It's a great way to shake yourself up. But it may be overfitting a bit. It might be at least as useful to ask yourself: if you could take a year off to work on something that probably wouldn't be important but would be really interesting, what would it be?  

### When to do what you love

What if you're not sure what you want? What if you're attracted to the idea of making money and more attracted to some kinds of work than others, but neither attraction predominates? How do you break ties?  

The key here is to understand that such ties are only apparent. When you have trouble choosing between following your interests and making money, it's never because you have complete knowledge of yourself and of the types of work you're choosing between, and the options are perfectly balanced. When you can't decide which path to take, it's almost always due to ignorance. In fact you're usually suffering from three kinds of ignorance simultaneously: you don't know what makes you happy, what the various kinds of work are really like, or how well you could do them.

What do you do in the face of uncertainty? Get more certainty. And probably the best way to do that is to try working on things you're interested in. That will get you more information about how interested you are in them, how good you are at them, and how much scope they offer for ambition.

Don't wait. Don't wait till the end of college to figure out what to work on. Don't even wait for internships during college. You don't necessarily need a job doing x in order to work on x; often you can just start doing it in some form yourself. And since figuring out what to work on is a problem that could take years to solve, the sooner you start, the better.  

One useful trick for judging different kinds of work is to look at who your colleagues will be. You'll become like whoever you work with. Do you want to become like these people?

The other thing you do in the face of uncertainty is to make choices that are uncertainty-proof. The less sure you are about what to do, the more important it is to choose options that give you more options in the future. I call this "staying upwind." If you're unsure whether to major in math or economics, for example, choose math; math is upwind of economics in the sense that it will be easier to switch later from math to economics than from economics to math.

There's one case, though, where it's easy to say whether you should work on what interests you the most: if you want to do ++[great work](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html)++. This is not a sufficient condition for doing great work, but it is a necessary one.  
There's a lot of selection bias in advice about whether to "follow your passion," and this is the reason. Most such advice comes from people who are famously successful, and if you ask someone who's famously successful how to do what they did, most will tell you that you have to work on what you're most interested in. And this is in fact true.  
That doesn't mean it's the right advice for everyone. Not everyone can do great work, or wants to. But if you do want to, the complicated question of whether or not to work on what interests you the most becomes simple. The answer is yes. The root of great work is a sort of ambitious curiosity, and you can't manufacture that.

## How to stuck in wrong career?

[https://herminiaibarra.com/how-to-stay-stuck-in-the-wrong-career/](https://herminiaibarra.com/how-to-stay-stuck-in-the-wrong-career/)

传统的职业变化方法——Susan的“逻辑”简历进展、Harris的人际关系和Gary的计划——都是我所说的“计划和实施”变革模式的一部分。事情是这样的：首先，尽可能清晰和确定你真正想做什么。接下来，利用这些知识来确定您的激情可以与您的技能和经验相结合的工作或领域。向最了解你的人和与市场保持一致的专业人士寻求建议。然后只需实施由此产生的行动步骤。变革被视为一次性交易：计划和实施方法警告我们不要在确切知道我们要去哪里之前采取行动。

这一切听起来都很合理，而且这是令人放心的前进方式。然而，我的研究表明，以这种方式进行将导致最灾难性的结果，也就是说没有结果。因此，如果你最深切的愿望是无限期地留在一个让你紧张或扼殺自我表达的职业中，只需坚持这种传统智慧，下面以万无一失的三点计划的形式呈现。

当然，回顾我们的职业生涯并确定我们喜欢和不喜欢什么，我们觉得满意和不满意的常见做法可以是一个有用的工具。但这种做法往往植根于一种深刻的误解，即有可能发现一个人的“真实自我”，而现实是，我们中没有人有这种本质。强烈的内省也构成了潜在的职业改变者会陷入白日梦的危险。要么幻想永远不会在现实世界中找到匹配的、能发工资的工作，要么与Gary不同，我们仍然在情感上依附于一个幻想的职业，而我们没有意识到我们已经长大了。

我们通过测试幻想和现实，而不是通过“向内看”来了解我们变成了谁——在实践中，而不是在理论上。了解自己至关重要，但它通常是重塑过程的结果，而不是第一个投入。更糟糕的是，从试图识别自己的真实自我开始，往往会导致瘫痪。当我们等待一闪而过的刺眼洞察力时，机会从我们身上而过。为了重新开始，我们需要摆脱我们的头脑。我们需要行动。

因此，如果自我评估、亲密人士的建议和变革专业人士的建议都做不到，那么我们在哪里可以找到重塑的支持？为了真正地与过去断裂，我们需要以新的眼光看待自己。我们需要去过那里并能理解我们要去哪里的导游。在我们正常圈子之外接触新的人、网络和专业社区是打破框架和获得心理寄託的最佳方式。

我们喜欢认为，我们可以直接从对变革的渴望跳到一个将完成我们重塑的单一决定——传统智慧是，你不应该用小的、肤浅的调整来愚弄自己。但试图过快地应对重大变化可能会适得其反。就像通过寻找真实的自我来开始过渡会导致瘫痪而不是进步一样，试图一劳永逸地做出重大举动可以阻止真正的改变。

有一种替代方法，它根据与计划和实施方法不同的逻辑工作。Gary、Harris和Susan，以及我观察到的许多其他成功的职业改变者，都分享了这种方法，我称之为“测试和学习”的变革模式。在过渡时期——当我们可能的自我正在疯狂转变时——创造变化的唯一方法是将我们可能的身份付诸实践，努力并塑造它们，直到它们充分基于经验来指导更决定性的步骤。

测试和学习方法认识到，应对不确定性和抵制熟悉因素的吸引力的唯一方法是使替代未来更加生动、更具体和更可行。我们在实践中获得了我们的旧身份。同样，在实践中，我们通过制定实验、转移联系和理解我们正在经历的变化来重新定义它们。这三种常见做法是最不同的职业变化的核心，为可能看起来像偶然发生和无序行为提供了逻辑。

制作实验是指创建这些附带项目的做法。他们最 大的优势是，我们可以在有限的范围内尝试新的专业角色，而不会影响我们目前的工作或不得不太快跳入新职位。在我观察到的几乎所有成功变革中，这个人已经深入参与了新职业相当长一段时间了。设置实验的方法有很多，这些方法都有效。苏珊刚刚下定决心探索一系列可能性，她接受了她旧工作中的自由职业任务，并为慈善机构做无端工作，作为她度过这个困难时期的生命线。其他人利用临时任务、外部合同、咨询工作和兼职来获得新行业的经验或培养技能。在新领域学习课程或获得培训和证书仍然是另一种实验方式。对于我研究中的许多人来说，执行计划、休假或延长假期提高了他们朝着新方向前进的能力。这些休息是强大的，因为它们迫使我们从日常工作中退出来，同时让我们与新的人和活动互动。

转移联系是指寻找能够帮助我们看到并成长为新自我的人的做法。对于我观察到的大多数成功的职业改变者来说，一个指导性人物或新的专业社区帮助照亮了道路，并缓冲了最终的飞跃。寻找一份新工作总是需要在我们通常圈子之外建立联系。我们通过拓展来获得想法和工作线索。例如，Gary非常成功地利用了他的校友和公司网络。

第三个工作身份实践是有意义的，指的是创造我们自己的变革触发器：为事件——重大和平凡——注入特殊意义，并将它们编织成一个关于我们正在成为谁的故事。从这些描述中很容易相信，职业变化是在这样的时刻产生的。但洞察力的时刻是变化的结果，而不是原因。在我的多次采访中，一个引人注目的发现是，这种时刻往往发生在过渡过程的后期，只有在经历了许多考验和磨难之后。定义时刻不是催化变化，而是帮助人们理解长期以来一直在展开的变化。好的故事在讲述和复述中发展，甚至在它们完全形成之前就被置于公共领域。例如，约翰没有因为拜访占星家而感到尴尬，而是向所有人讲述了他的故事，甚至在报纸专栏中写下了这个故事。他越接近找到自己的创造性出口，这一集就越有意义，他的故事就越少引发“你为什么要那样做？”反应。通过公开宣布我们所寻求的东西以及连接我们旧自我和新自我的共同线索，我们澄清了我们的意图，并提高了我们获得他人支持的能力。

## Career Craftsman Manifesto [https://calnewport.com/the-career-craftsman-manifesto/](https://calnewport.com/the-career-craftsman-manifesto/)

***The Career Craftsman*** understands that “follow your passion and all will be happy” is a children’s tale. Most people don’t have pre-existing passions waiting to be unearthed. Happiness requires more than solving a simple matching problem.

***The Career Craftsman*** knows there’s no magical “right job” waiting out there for you. Any number of pursuits can provide the foundation for an engaging life.

***The Career Craftsman*** believes that compelling careers are not courageously pursued or serendipitously discovered, but are instead systematically crafted.

***The Career Craftsman*** believes this process of *career crafting* always begins with the mastery of something rare and valuable. The traits that define great work (autonomy, creativity, impact, recognition) are rare and valuable themselves, and you need something to offer in return. Put another way: no one owes you a fulfilling job; you have to earn it.

***The Career Craftsman*** believes that mastery is just the first step in crafting work you love. Once you have the leverage of a rare and valuable skill, you need to apply this leverage strategically to make your working life increasingly fulfulling. It is then — *and only then* — that you should expect a feeling of passion for your work to truly take hold.

***The Career Craftsman*** thinks the idea that “societal expectations” are trying to hold you down in a safe but boring career path is a boogeyman invented to sell eBooks. You don’t need courage to create a cool life. You need the type of valuable skills that let you write your own ticket.

***The Career Craftsman*** never expects to love an entry level job (or to stay in that job long before moving up).

***The Career Craftsman*** thinks “is this my calling?” is a stupid question.

***The Career Craftsman*** is data-driven. Admire someone’s career? Work out *exactly* how they made it happen. The answers you’ll find will be less romantic but more actionable than you might expect.

***The Career Craftsman*** believes the color of your parachute is irrelevant if you take the time to get good at flying the damn plane in the first place.

# On Passion and Its Discontents

I detail two reasons why “follow your passion” is bad advice:

- ***The first reason*** is that most people don’t have a clear pre-defined passion to follow. This is especially true if you consider young people who are just setting out on their own for the first time. The advice to “follow your passion” is frustratingly meaningless if, like many people, you don’t have a passion to follow.
- ***The second reason*** is that we don’t have much evidence that matching your job to a pre-existing interest makes you more likely to find that work satisfying. The properties we know lead people to enjoy their work — [such as autonomy, mastery, and relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination_theory) — have little to do with whether or not the work matches an established inclination.

What works better? Put in the hard work to master something rare and valuable, then deploy this leverage to steer your working life in directions that resonate.
